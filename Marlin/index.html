Marlin and Nemo

Overview

For the final project in my AE 3030 (Aerodynamics) course at Georgia Tech, I designed and built two devices — a fully functional RC plane (Marlin) and an unpowered glider (Nemo) — to demonstrate the effects of different airfoils. Marlin featured a custom-designed, milled PCB with an Arduino Nano and HC-12 radio for flight control, while Nemo was a smaller glider with interchangeable wings.

Marlin — The RC Plane

I designed a custom PCB using KiCAD to integrate an Arduino Nano and HC-12 radio module (433 MHz) for flight control. The PCB was milled using the Invention Studio's PCB mill, leveraging my experience with FlatCAM to generate precise G-code. I adapted ground control code from my QuadFrog project to work with the Arduino Nano. The airframe was designed in SolidWorks and fabricated using 3D-printed PLA components.

Nemo — The Glider

Nemo was designed as a smaller, unpowered glider with interchangeable wings featuring different airfoil profiles (NACA 2412, flat-bottom, inverted). The wings attached via a slot-and-tab system, allowing quick swaps during demonstrations. During testing, Nemo showcased aerodynamic phenomena like roll induced by an upside-down wing.

Results

Both devices met the project's goals, earning positive feedback from my instructor for their functionality and creativity. My unique contribution was the custom PCB for Marlin, which integrated the Arduino Nano and HC-12 radio, enabling adaptable control code that could be reused across various plane designs.
