RoboJackets URC Drone

URC 2025

As Drone Lead, I overhauled our entire system from scratch, focusing on maintaining communications since that was our point of failure in 2024. I rebuilt the frame using scrapped carbon fiber components from a Tarot frame integrated with a custom 3D-printed shell.

Communications System

To solve the HAB operational range issue, I designed and built a tracking antenna using an Intel NUC to receive telemetry and video and relay it into the ground station. For telemetry I used RFD900x radios. For high-quality aerial video, I switched from analog to digital using OpenHD with a Raspberry Pi 4, Arducam camera, and ASUS AC56 WiFi adapter powered directly from the PDB.

Competition Results

EEVI had a successful autonomous takeoff and began a stable flight towards the target. Unfortunately, GPS terrain data was not properly implemented and EEVI collided with a hill between the ground station and target. Despite the crash, the video feed remained stable with only half a second of latency, and all electronics were fully intact. Looking forward, I want to include more distance sensor integration and rework the frame design.

URC 2024

Building the First Drone

As part of the RoboNav team at RoboJackets, we competed at the University Rover Challenge in Utah. The drone subteam consisted of just me and one other member. We were given parts from a broken drone from old members and set out to build something capable of assisting the rover.

Technical Approach

The heart was a Pixhawk running ArduPilot. We integrated an end effector for transporting hand-tools, allowing the rover to focus on larger tasks. I handled the electrical and software integration, using a custom PCB from a different project to supply power and PWM to the servos.

Lessons Learned

Shortly before competition, we realized our telemetry range was too limited. I mounted the receiver on the rover itself and relayed data through its more reliable communications. Unfortunately, a faulty wire connection at competition prevented video, but we were proud of what we accomplished and excited for version 2.
