Custom Remote

About

This project was a personal exploration of mine into RF modules and PCB fabrication. I wanted to have a device that would be able to selectively activate any device which could be easily outfitted with a matching module, or by a MOSFET switch controlled by the RF module. I was able to explore various libraries for RF communication and I enjoyed testing out the RF module hardware. I decided it was important to make the resulting circuit as compact as possible which meant it needed to be put onto a PCB. The opportunity to fabricate my own PCB was an important component of this project as it was my responsibility as Electronics Apprentice to make a procedure for a new PCB mill acquired by the Invention Studio. I applied my experience with KiCAD and learned how to use FlatCAM so I could make this project as a demo board for the new mill.

This project was a personal exploration into RF modules and PCB fabrication, driven by my desire to create a versatile remote control capable of selectively activating devices equipped with compatible RF modules or MOSFET switches. The goal was to design a compact, reliable circuit that demonstrates the power of RF communication while showcasing my skills in PCB design and fabrication, using my role and access to equipment as an Electronics Apprentice at the Invention Studio.
This project allowed me to dive into RF communication and its use for portable and modular controls in any project. By designing a compact remote and fabricating a custom PCB, I aimed to enhance the capabilites of any future projects by providing an existing standard that could be easily implemented.

The technical core was the RF module integration. I explored various RF communication libraries, experimenting with protocols to ensure reliable signal transmission. Using KiCAD, I designed a schematic for a microcontroller-based circuit that interfaced with an RF module and supported MOSFET switch control for device activation.
To achieve compactness, I fabricated a custom PCB using the Invention Studio's new PCB mill. As Electronics Apprentice, I developed a procedure for the mill, learning FlatCAM to convert my KiCAD designs into machine-readable G-code. This process involved iterative testing to perfect milling parameters, ensuring clean traces and reliable boards. The final PCB was a demo board for the mill, showcasing its capabilities while meeting the project's size constraints.


The enclosure, designed in SolidWorks and 3D-printed, was tailored to fit the compact PCB while maintaining accessibility for user controls and a simple and easy area to view the user OLED display.
The final remote successfully activated devices equipped with matching RF modules or MOSFET switches, with testing confirming reliable performance across a 10-meter range. The compact PCB design and custom enclosure made the device portable and user-friendly.
