## Overview

The third version of my Kermit series of drones, Kermit V3 focused on more sensors and portability. Inspired first by the DJI Mavic Pro and then mostly by the Skydio X10, I wanted to integrate a 3-axis stabilized gimbal, ground sensing and surround obstacle detection — all in a package I could fold up and take with me on the go.

## Stage 1 — sensors and a dense frame

I split the project into two stages: sensor integration and general frame design first, then frame improvements and the folding mechanism. For the stage-1 frame I kept things as compact as I could, packing the large components next to each other in a small, dense frame. That traded away the usual modularity of wider plate-based frames like the F405.

## Stage 2 — folding arms

Building and flying the stage-1 quad showed me plenty to improve, especially deployment and ease of access. Because of where the arm mounts sit, I went with a **double, vertical-flip arm**: it kept the design simple and strong, and very compact when folded.

## The completed build

The finished drone carries a 360° lidar, a Here4 GPS, the SIYI A8 mini gimbal with a 4K camera, the Holybro H-Flow lidar + optical flow sensor, and a long-range radio that talks to the handheld radio/tablet ground station beside it.

## Flight testing

Test flying went well, though the PID gains still need more tuning. The 3-axis gimbal worked very well despite the untuned gains, and the added sensors gave great information about the drone's surroundings — integrating them in ArduPilot was its own interesting lesson.

## Three versions in

I'm pleased with how the project has progressed across its three versions:

- **[Kermit V1](/projects/quadfrog)** was my introduction to building custom drones. Small and simple, it taught me basic frame design, Pixhawk and ArduPilot, and companion-computer integration.
- **[Kermit V2](/projects/kermit-v2)** was a major frame upgrade, with carbon fiber tubes for the arms and legs, basic autonomous navigation through ArduPilot with a new GPS, and much better telemetry with an onboard camera via OpenHD on a Raspberry Pi 4.
- **Kermit V3** is the most refined: a streamlined, functional frame that shrinks both the main body and the overall footprint with a folding mechanism, and much better sensor fusion from a better GPS, a 360° lidar and a ground-facing optical flow module.
