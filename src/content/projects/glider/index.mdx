## Overview

In AE 1601 (Intro to Aerospace Engineering), my first year, I designed and built a glider to explore the fundamentals of glider design and how each feature affects performance. The project ended in a distance competition — which my design won.

## Design process

Over several weeks the professor introduced design concepts one at a time — aspect ratio, taper, sweep. Balsa for the final build was limited, so I turned to the Invention Studio's laser cutters and a lot of discarded cardboard to prototype freely.

## Iteration & testing

I tried feature after feature and learned from every crash. Early prototypes showed wing-stability problems — some fluttered on launch — so I adjusted the dihedral angle and added a stabilizing tail. Each prototype was hand-launched and its distance recorded to quantify improvements. Custom 3D-printed parts set a precise dihedral angle and added a hub that shifted the center of mass forward.

## Competition

At the competition my throws were consistent and long, and the glider won. It was a great first taste of the design–build–test loop in my aerospace major.
