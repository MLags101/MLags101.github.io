## Overview

As a Prototyping Instructor at the Invention Studio, I pursued the role of **Electronics Master**. The final task was to design and build something that makes the space better. I built a lockbox to secure the electronics cabinet keys — integrating RFID, an RF remote, a solenoid lock and a spark-gap deterrent.

### Motivation

The electronics tool group is a small family of engineers. The lockbox gave all of us access with our student IDs, so special equipment could be locked up without key access becoming a burden.

<Figure src="img-6131.jpg" alt="Lockbox electronics: Arduino Nano on a milled board with RFID reader and solenoid" caption="The control electronics before installation." />

## Technical features

- **RFID reader** — an MFRC522 module (13.56 MHz) reads university ID cards over MIFARE. The Arduino checks card UIDs against an authorized list stored in EEPROM.
- **MOSFET-driven solenoid** — a 12 V solenoid retracts when an authorized ID or RF signal is detected.
- **RF module** — a 433 MHz receiver allows remote unlocking for convenient key access.
- **Spark-gap deterrent** — an incorrect ID fires a loud arc from a high-voltage transformer driven by a PWM-controlled MOSFET.

## Fabrication

I designed a 3D-printed door and laser-cut MDF sides in SolidWorks, iterating on prototypes to fix component fit and solenoid alignment until the lock operated smoothly.

## Results

The lockbox secured the cabinet keys reliably: IDs authenticated, the RF remote worked, and the spark gap fired consistently on unauthorized attempts. The tool group adopted it — and it earned me the Electronics Master role.
