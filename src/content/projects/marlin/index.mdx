## Overview

For the final project in AE 3030 (Aerodynamics) at Georgia Tech, I designed and built two aircraft to demonstrate the effects of different airfoils: **Marlin**, a fully functional RC plane, and **Nemo**, an unpowered glider with interchangeable wings. Marlin flew on a custom milled PCB carrying an Arduino Nano and an HC-12 radio.

## Marlin — the RC plane

I designed a custom PCB in KiCad to integrate the Arduino Nano and HC-12 radio (433 MHz) for flight control, and milled it on the Invention Studio's PCB mill — using my FlatCAM experience to generate the G-code. I adapted the ground-control code from QuadFrog to run on the Nano. The airframe was designed in SolidWorks and printed in PLA.

## Nemo — the glider

Nemo is a smaller, unpowered glider whose wings swap between three airfoils — **NACA 2412**, **flat-bottom** and **inverted** — via a slot-and-tab joint, so they could be changed quickly mid-demo. In testing it showed the effects clearly, like the roll induced by an upside-down wing.

## Results

Both aircraft met the project goals and earned strong feedback for function and creativity. My particular contribution was Marlin's custom PCB — a reusable flight board whose control code adapts to other airframes.
