## Why build one

I started this project to fill a gap in small-UAV prototyping: a compact, low-cost brushless controller made for camera gimbals. Existing options were either expensive and oversized (ODrive, Moteus) or plagued with software issues (STorM32).

<Figure src="storm.jpg" alt="STorM32 gimbal controller board" caption="The STorM32 gimbal controller." contain />

## V1 — Teensy + L6234

I began by researching brushless control principles and studying the open-source schematics of the STorM32, ODrive and Moteus. The first board tested the **L6234** three-phase driver with a Teensy generating the control signals. I drew the schematic from the datasheet and had the board fabricated by JLCPCB.

### Testing & setback

Assembly was hard without a reflow oven — I hand-soldered every SMD part. Initial results were promising, but before I could go further I accidentally shorted the power supply through the microcontroller and destroyed my only Teensy. I took what had worked and moved straight to a fully integrated design.

<Figure src="img-7389.jpg" alt="Assembled V1 driver board with the Teensy attached" caption="V1, assembled and on the bench." />

<Callout tone="lesson">
With only one Teensy, one short stopped testing. V2 puts the microcontroller on the board itself.
</Callout>

## V2 — integrated ATmega328

The current revision puts an **ATmega328** directly on a four-layer PCB (copper on both faces plus two inner layers) alongside the L6234 to maximize compactness. The schematic was informed by ElectroNoobs' work on sensorless, slow-speed brushless control.

I assembled and tested the boards in the Student Competition Center. After some troubleshooting they proved effective at **velocity and position control** of the gimbal's brushless motor, and they're now integrated into EEVi v3.
