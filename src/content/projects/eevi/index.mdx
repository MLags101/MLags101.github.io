## URC 2025

As Drone Lead, I overhauled our entire system from scratch, focusing on keeping communications alive — that had been our point of failure in 2024. I rebuilt the frame from scrapped carbon fiber components of a Tarot frame, with parametric SolidWorks parts and a custom 3D-printed shell for desert operation, and checked the landing gear's structural margins with FEA in ANSYS Mechanical.

### Communications system

To solve the operational-range problem, I designed and built a **2-axis auto-tracking antenna** in SolidWorks running ArduPilot, with an Intel NUC to receive telemetry and video and relay them into the ground station. The tracker extended our usable comms range by about 2 km.

For telemetry I moved to RFD900x radios. For aerial video I switched from analog to digital, using OpenHD on a Raspberry Pi 4 with an Arducam camera and an ASUS AC56 Wi-Fi adapter powered directly from the PDB.

### Competition results

EEVi made a successful **autonomous takeoff** and began a stable flight toward the target. Unfortunately, GPS terrain data wasn't set up correctly and EEVi flew into a hill between the ground station and the target. Even so, the video feed stayed stable with only about half a second of latency, and every electronic component came through intact.

## URC 2024

As part of RoboNav at RoboJackets, we competed at the University Rover Challenge in Utah. The drone subteam was just me and one other member. We were handed parts from a broken drone left by former members and set out to build something that could help the rover.

### Technical approach

The heart of the build was a Pixhawk running ArduPilot. We integrated an end effector for carrying hand tools, so the rover could stay focused on larger tasks. I handled the electrical and software integration, reusing a custom PCB from another project to supply power and PWM to the servos.

<Figure src="img-6374.jpg" alt="The 2024 drone on a table next to a laptop running the ground control software" caption="Final checks on the 2024 build." />

### Lessons learned

Shortly before competition we realized our telemetry range was too limited. I mounted the receiver on the rover itself and relayed data through its more reliable communications link. A faulty wire at competition still cost us video — but the team was proud of what two people built, and it set the requirements for the 2025 rebuild.
